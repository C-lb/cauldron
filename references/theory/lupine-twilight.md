# Lupine Twilight

> a violet bloom over near-black foliage

Five named swatches: Laurel Green, Rhythm, Camouflage Green, Ebony, Eerie Black. One
violet bloom, three stacked greens running from sage to near-black, one true-black anchor.
A botanical-photography kit — the flower gets one voice, the foliage does everything else.

## Harmony

Laurel Green, Camouflage Green and Ebony sit inside a tight 12° band around 95–100°, all
yellow-green at descending value and saturation (63%/31%, 47%/26%, 29%/22%). That's the
"foliage" run: three depths of the same leaf, not three different colours. Eerie Black sits
at 220°, technically a cool hue but so close to zero saturation (9%) that it reads as
neutral — it's the anchor, not a sixth colour.

Rhythm is the outlier at 249°, a genuine violet roughly 150° from the green cluster. Unlike
Larkspur Orchard's accent, Rhythm isn't far brighter than its neighbours — at 62% value and
33% saturation it's close in weight to Camouflage Green, so it doesn't leap out by contrast.
It leaps out by being the only hue in the set that isn't green. That's a quieter kind of
accent: a kit meant for a photograph where the flower is already the subject and the palette
just needs to not fight it.

## What Rhythm does perceptually

It measures 3.80:1 against Eerie Black — large text and UI chrome only, well short of AA
body. It is not a text colour in this kit; it's a fill, a rule, an illustrated petal. Used at
high saturation or over a large field it stops reading as "the flower in the frame" and
starts reading as a generic purple brand accent, which throws away the botanical specificity
that makes the kit work.

Measured against its nearest neighbour, Camouflage Green, it sits at only 1.41:1 — a
near-isoluminant pair despite the 150° hue gap. That confirms the read above: Rhythm carries
the kit by hue alone, not by standing out in brightness, so it needs a clean edge (a
photograph's natural silhouette, an illustrated outline) rather than expecting the eye to
find it by lightness contrast.

## The green run, and the problem with it

Laurel Green, Camouflage Green and Ebony step down in even-ish jumps (1.98:1 driven mostly
by Laurel Green vs. Rhythm, then 1.85:1 Camouflage Green/Ebony), which is closer to usable
separation than Larkspur Orchard's pastel run or Lemnian Hollow's mid-tone cluster. Still,
all three share the same hue family, so a legend or chart that relies on "green A vs green B
vs green C" is asking colour-blind viewers and small-scale reproductions to do more work than
this kit can support alone — pair with position or a label whenever the distinction matters.

Ebony against the anchor is the weak link: 2.90:1, which fails outright. Ebony is functionally
a second dark, useful as texture near the anchor but not as a swatch expected to read against
it.

## The anchor

Eerie Black is genuinely near-black (9% saturation, low value) and holds the full range —
AAA against Laurel Green, AA against Camouflage Green, chrome-only against Rhythm, failing
against Ebony. It's the only swatch built to carry long-form text or a full-bleed ground.

## Where it breaks

**Children's products and anything that needs to read as bright or friendly.** This is a
dusk palette — moody and quiet by design. Forcing it into a cheerful brief fights the whole
premise.

**Dense data tables and status UI.** Three greens sharing one hue family plus a violet that
barely separates from its neighbour in lightness — nothing here is built to carry state at a
glance.

## Applying it

Surface Eerie Black, or let a real photograph stand in for it. Run the green trio as
foliage — fills, illustration, texture — and spend Rhythm on the one thing that's meant to
be the flower: an accent shape, a link, a single bloom. Don't scale Rhythm to a full
background; it loses its specificity the moment it stops reading as "the one different
thing in the frame."

Body text: on Eerie Black, Laurel Green clears AAA and Camouflage Green clears AA; Rhythm and
Ebony are chrome/large-text only or fail outright. This kit has no light surface among its
five swatches — bring in an off-white if the design needs body copy on a pale ground.
