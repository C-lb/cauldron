# Matcha Ceremony

> bright matcha settling into pale bamboo

Four named swatches: Bamboo Beige, Tea Mist, Matcha Lime, Forest Brew. Two pale neutrals,
one saturated lime-green, one near-black anchor. A tea-and-wellness kit with an unusually
high contrast ceiling for how few colours it has — every swatch except the anchor clears AAA
against it.

## Harmony

All four swatches sit inside a 65–80° yellow-green band — this kit doesn't have Larkspur
Orchard's or Lupine Twilight's cross-hue accent; it's a single hue family from near-white to
near-black. Matcha Lime carries almost all the saturation (81%), against 25% for Bamboo Beige
and 22% for Tea Mist. Forest Brew sits at 75% saturation but 12% value — Matcha Lime's own
hue, driven dark rather than diluted, the same anchor move Ember Hollow and Lupine Twilight
both make.

Because everything shares one hue family, this kit reads as depth rather than as contrast —
matcha powder, steeped tea, and the bowl it's served in, all recognisably the same plant at
different concentrations.

## What Matcha Lime does perceptually

At 81% saturation it's more intense than anything in Larkspur Orchard, Lupine Twilight, or
Paprika Bazaar, and by a wide margin the most saturated swatch on the shelf outside
Strawberry Bramble's Tamarillo. It clears AAA body against Forest Brew (7.41:1) despite being
this vivid, which is unusual — most kits trade saturation for contrast, and this one doesn't
have to, because Forest Brew is dark enough in value to hold the line regardless.

That headroom means Matcha Lime can carry more weight than a typical single-accent swatch —
it's usable as a real fill, not just a decorative pop — but it's still the kit's one loud
voice among three quiet ones. Used everywhere, it turns "tea ceremony" into "highlighter,"
so it still wants rationing even though the numbers allow more.

## The pale pair, and the problem with it

Bamboo Beige and Tea Mist are near-isoluminant (1.08:1), and Tea Mist/Matcha Lime is also
near-isoluminant (1.22:1) despite the visible saturation jump — lightness and saturation are
different axes, and this kit's neutrals prove it. Both pale swatches clear AAA against Forest
Brew individually (9.82:1 and 9.07:1), so the risk isn't legibility on the dark anchor — it's
that Bamboo Beige and Tea Mist are hard to tell apart from each other on their own, and both
are close enough to Matcha Lime in lightness that a design leaning on lightness alone to
separate the three will struggle.

## The anchor

Forest Brew is the only genuinely dark swatch (12% value) and it's doing more work than its
lightness alone suggests — because it shares Matcha Lime's hue rather than being a neutral
black, dark-mode or footer treatments built from it read as "the tea gone dark," not as a
black box behind green art. It holds AAA against all three lighter swatches, which is the
highest anchor ceiling of any kit on the shelf.

## Where it breaks

**High-contrast dark UI.** Ironically, despite the anchor's strong numbers, three of the four
swatches are pale and low-saturation — a dark-mode interface built from this kit would be
starved for chromatic material once Matcha Lime is spent.

**Dense data tables and medical alerting.** One hue family, no semantic register, and two
near-isoluminant pale swatches. Nothing here is built to carry state at a glance.

## Applying it

Surface Bamboo Beige or Tea Mist for the pale ground — they're close enough that either works
as the base, not both trying to do different jobs. Spend Matcha Lime as the one saturated
element: a button, an icon, a highlight — it has the contrast headroom to go slightly further
than a typical accent, but not so far it stops reading as "the vivid one." Reserve Forest
Brew for body text and the one dark surface the kit supports; every other swatch clears AAA
against it.
