# Paprika Bazaar

> paprika and honey fading into pale crumpet

Four named swatches: Hot Paprika, Honeycomb, Biscuit, Crumpet. One saturated red anchor,
three warm neutrals stepping up to near-white. A bakery-and-spice kit with the darkest
swatch doing the work most kits ask of their lightest — Hot Paprika is both the anchor and
the only real colour in the set.

## Harmony

All four swatches sit inside a narrow 30–40° amber-to-red band. Hot Paprika is the outlier
by saturation (68%) and the only swatch dark enough to anchor anything; Honeycomb, Biscuit
and Crumpet run 55% → 33% → 17% saturation at ascending value, which is a single warm-neutral
ramp rather than three distinct colours. This kit doesn't have Larkspur Orchard's "pastel
run plus one saturated accent" shape — it has a saturated anchor plus a fading-out neutral
ramp, closer to a monochrome tint scale than a multi-hue kit.

## What Hot Paprika does perceptually

It's the darkest swatch and the only one with real chroma, so it plays both roles other kits
split across two swatches: the anchor that holds contrast, and the accent that carries hue.
That dual duty is the kit's defining constraint. Use it as a ground and it reads as "spice
market wall"; use it as a small accent against Crumpet and it reads as "paprika dusted on
pastry." It cannot comfortably be both a full-bleed background and a small pop in the same
composition — pick one register per design.

Measured against its own neutrals it clears AA body against Crumpet (4.81:1) but nothing
better — there's no swatch in this kit that clears AAA against Hot Paprika, which is unusual
for an anchor this saturated. That's because Hot Paprika's own value isn't especially low
(its darkness comes from saturation, not from being near-black); it doesn't have the contrast
range of a true near-black like Licorice or Eerie Black.

## The neutral ramp, and the problem with it

Crumpet/Biscuit (1.42:1) and Biscuit/Honeycomb (1.18:1) are both near-isoluminant. Visually
that's correct for the brief — this is meant to read as one continuous bake, crumpet fading
into biscuit fading into honeycomb — but it means none of the three can be told apart from
its neighbour without a label, a border, or very different amounts of area. A three-way
legend built from Crumpet, Biscuit and Honeycomb will not survive greyscale or a glance.

Only Honeycomb/Hot Paprika breaks that pattern (2.87:1), and even that fails AA. This kit has
exactly one hard edge — where the neutral ramp meets the anchor — everything else is a soft
gradient.

## Where it breaks

**Cool minimalist tech.** The palette is warm to the point of being entirely amber-to-red;
there's no cool swatch anywhere to balance it, so it fights any brief asking for restraint or
neutrality.

**Dense data tables.** Three near-isoluminant neutrals plus one anchor that can't clear AAA
against anything gives this kit the shallowest contrast ceiling on the shelf. It's built for
a warm wash across a bakery or spice-market brand, not for distinguishing rows or states.

## Applying it

Surface Crumpet or Biscuit for the pale ground. Let Honeycomb sit as a mid transition — a
divider, a hover state, texture between the pale ramp and the anchor. Reserve Hot Paprika for
whichever single job the design needs most: either the one saturated ground, or the one
accent against the pale neutrals, not both at once.

Body text: on Hot Paprika, Crumpet clears AA and Biscuit is large-text/UI only; Honeycomb
fails outright. This kit has no true near-black — if the design needs a genuinely dark
surface for long-form text, bring one in from outside the kit.
