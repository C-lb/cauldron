# Strawberry Bramble

> sage leaves and tart red over dark kelp

Four named swatches, arriving already labelled Primary/Primary/Secondary/Secondary: Kelp,
Willow Grove, Whiskey, Tamarillo. Two muted greens standing in for foliage, one warm tan,
one saturated red standing in for the fruit. A produce-photography kit built around a single
sharp contrast — the one bright thing on the bramble.

## Harmony

Willow Grove and Kelp share a hue family at 92–96°, yellow-green, stepping down from 34%
value/22% saturation to 13% value/9% saturation — leaf to shadow, the same move Larkspur
Orchard and Lupine Twilight both make with their anchors. Whiskey sits at 21°, a warm tan
with real saturation (48%). Tamarillo is the outlier at 2°, essentially pure red at 74%
saturation — by far the most intense swatch in the kit and the only one that reads as fruit
rather than surroundings.

That's the structure: two greens playing the same role at different depths, one warm neutral,
and one loud red doing all the work. Unlike Lupine Twilight's Rhythm, Tamarillo doesn't need
a lightness argument to carry the accent — it wins on saturation alone, 74% against a next-
highest of 48%.

## What Tamarillo does perceptually

It's the reason this kit exists. At 74% saturation it's more intense than anything in Ember
Hollow, Larkspur Orchard, or Lupine Twilight, and it should be spent exactly like the
Tamarillo/fruit it's named for — small, punctuated, surrounded by green. A field of Tamarillo
reads as alarm-red before it reads as strawberry, because high-saturation red is the one hue
every status system reaches for first.

It also measures the worst of the four against the anchor: 1.26:1 on Kelp, which fails
outright and is near-isoluminant with the anchor itself. That's not a flaw to design around
so much as a fact to route around — Tamarillo needs a light ground (Whiskey, or an actual
photograph) to read as a colour at all. Against Kelp it just reads as "slightly warmer
black."

## The rest of the kit, and the problem with it

Whiskey is the only swatch that clears anything against Kelp (4.43:1, large text / UI only)
and is this kit's de facto light surface — there's no true off-white or cream here, unlike
Larkspur Orchard or Paprika Bazaar. If the design needs body copy on a pale ground, Whiskey
is what's available, and even it doesn't clear AA body.

Willow Grove fails against Kelp outright (2.46:1) despite being visibly a different green —
the two share a hue family and sit closer in lightness than the eye expects. Treat the
green pair the way Lupine Twilight treats its green trio: fine as layered fills and texture,
not as a legend distinguishing two states from each other.

## The anchor

Kelp is dark but not deeply so (13% value) — it's the darkest member by a wide margin, but
it's not a true near-black the way Eerie Black or Licorice are. That's why every ratio in this
kit reads lower than in the other kits on the shelf: there's no swatch here that reaches a
near-black anchor's contrast ceiling. Nothing in Strawberry Bramble clears AAA body against
anything else in it.

## Where it breaks

**Fintech, medical, high-density dashboards.** No swatch here clears AA body against the
anchor except Whiskey at large-text-only strength, and the kit's whole ceiling is lower than
the rest of the shelf. This is a produce-photography accent kit, not a system for setting
long paragraphs.

**Anything needing more than one alert colour.** Tamarillo is doing double duty as "the
brand's one saturated hue" and would also be the obvious pick for "danger" in a semantic
system — using it for both in the same interface muddies the read.

## Applying it

Surface Whiskey where the design needs to read at all — it's the only swatch with real
contrast headroom. Layer Willow Grove and Kelp as foliage and shadow, not as two states that
need telling apart. Spend Tamarillo the way a photograph spends a strawberry: small, against
a lighter ground, surrounded by green. Never let it carry body text; it clears nothing on
this kit's own anchor.
